The CONGEST model

The CONGEST model is the LOCAL model with limited bandwidth: the nodes of the input graph compute in synchronous rounds, but in each round a node can send only a message of $O(\log n)$ bits, a constant number of identifiers, over each of its edges. The complexity of an algorithm is again the number of rounds until every node knows its part of the output. A node can no longer collect its whole neighborhood quickly, and information that has to pass through a few edges queues up there.

The table lists the best known lower and upper bound for every problem on general graphs, for deterministic and randomized algorithms. Only algorithms that work with $O(\log n)$-bit messages count, while every lower bound for LOCAL also holds here. Runtimes depend on the number of nodes $n$ and the maximum degree $\Delta$: choose a regime of $\Delta$ to compare them as functions of $n$, or take the worst case over all $\Delta$.

Bounds are taken from the results in the database and compared at sample values of Δ, so they can only be as complete as the database. Corrections are welcome on the problem pages.
