Problem

Sinkless orientation

A sinkless orientation of a graph $G = (V, E)$ orients every edge so that every node has at least one outgoing edge, that is, no node is a sink. It always exists if every node has degree at least $3$, but cannot be found locally on cycles and paths. Sinkless orientation was the first problem shown to be exponentially easier with randomness than without, and its lower bound was the first proof by round elimination.

Runtimes are given in rounds, in terms of the number of nodes $n$ and the degree $\Delta \ge 3$: the algorithms work on all graphs of minimum degree $\Delta$, the lower bounds hold on $\Delta$-regular graphs.
