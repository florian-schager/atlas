Model

The LOCAL model

The input graph is the communication network: every node is a processor with a unique identifier, and the nodes compute in synchronous rounds. In each round, every node sends a message of unbounded size to each of its neighbors, receives theirs and computes; in the randomized setting it also has private random bits. The complexity of an algorithm is the number of rounds until every node knows its part of the output. Since messages are unrestricted (unlike in the CONGEST model), a $t$-round algorithm is exactly a function from radius-$t$ neighborhoods to outputs.

The table lists the best known lower and upper bound for every problem on general graphs, for deterministic and randomized algorithms. Runtimes depend on the number of nodes $n$ and the maximum degree $\Delta$: choose a regime of $\Delta$ to compare them as functions of $n$, or take the worst case over all $\Delta$.

Bounds are taken from the results in the database and compared at sample values of Δ, so they can only be as complete as the database. Corrections are welcome on the problem pages.
